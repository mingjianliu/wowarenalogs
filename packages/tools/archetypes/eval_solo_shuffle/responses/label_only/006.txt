This 2:44 loss reflects the heavy_burst_swap pattern at its most demanding: four burst windows spread across the full match, 52% dampening, and a healer who needed to absorb repeated swap-driven spikes while managing an increasingly depleted toolkit. The Mistweaver Monk's overall cooldown economy was stressed but not broken — the loss came down to a single moment where all defensive options ran dry simultaneously.

Life Cocoon was used twice, at 0:23 and 2:05. The first use at 0:23 came very early when the Warriors HP was at 90% and neither the Mage nor Warrior were yet critically low, responding to an early Bestial Wrath window. That 120-second investment set the second charge on a long recovery cycle, meaning it was not available again until well past the 1:23 critical window. At 2:05, with the second Bestial Wrath + Explosive Shot window open and the shaman at 32%, the second Life Cocoon fired correctly and visibly stabilized the team. The problem is 52% dampening means each Life Cocoon absorbs roughly half what it would at 0% — the shield value was severely compromised in the late game.

Leg Sweep saw three charges across the match: 0:30 on the paladin at full health during low urgency, 1:23 on both hunters while the Mage was at 37% during an active burst window, and 2:26 on only the MM Hunter right before the final Bestial Wrath + Trueshot window at 2:33. The 1:23 cast was the best use — two targets, active kill attempt, with Combustion running and the Mage critically low. The 2:26 cast was the most costly: spending the Leg Sweep charge on a single target 7 seconds before the deadliest window of the match, then entering that window with no stun available when the Mage sat at 15–22% HP for seven seconds and eventually died to Wailing Arrow (119k) plus pet damage.

The cleanse at 1:03, removing the debuff off the Mage at 53% during the first Trueshot window, was timely and likely saved the Mage from dying in that window. This is the kind of proactive utility that kept the match competitive through the mid-game. However, the Paralysis uses in this match were less visible in the log — the 0:42 Paralysis cast appears as a freecast during a recovery period rather than as a CC to interrupt an incoming burst. In future against this composition, Paralysis on the Paladin healer before their Avenging Wrath window at 1:24 would have been more impactful than using it reactively off cooldown.

The final sequence from 2:35 to 2:43 shows the accumulated CC debt: Blinding Light on the Monk at 2:35 for 4 seconds, followed by Scorpid Venom, Spider Venom, and Binding Shot in quick succession, with the Monk's trinket not available for 89 more seconds. During those eight seconds the Mage sat between 15% and 22% while Trueshot fired at 2:41. The trinket was used at 2:41 to break Spider Venom, but by then the Mage was at 20% and the next attack killed her. Against double-hunter into heavy_burst_swap archetypes, the Monk must track when both Bestial Wrath and Trueshot align, save Leg Sweep and Paralysis for that specific window, and consider accepting smaller burst windows without stuns to ensure the synchronized CD window can be disrupted.
