The fast_burst_swap win pattern is exactly what happened here: the team pre-committed Avatar into the enemy's Bestial Wrath + Combustion window, drove the Fire Mage to dead in 14 seconds, and the Mistweaver Monk kept the team stable throughout without spending a single major cooldown. This is the archetype's clean-win template.

The round was decided in a single 15-second sequence from 0:27 to 0:42. Avatar came out at 0:26 when the Warrior was at 97% — not a reactive call, but a proactive investment timed to the anticipated enemy CD rotation. Bestial Wrath followed at 0:27, Combustion at 0:28. The enemy committed their full offensive toolkit into Avatar's window, which meant the Warrior took reduced damage during the peak, while simultaneously the team's damage output compounded with the enemy Fire Mage taking 16% in two seconds (0:29–0:30), 10% more in the next two (0:31–0:32). The Fire Mage was at 4% at 0:41 and dead at 0:42.

The Monk's healing through this window was efficient and controlled. The team dipped to 85–88% across all three players at 0:31–0:34 under Bestial Wrath pressure, but the Monk stabilized without Life Cocoon or Paralysis. The enemy Holy Paladin could not save the Fire Mage — the kill happened too quickly for a Paladin with no pre-committed defensive CD to respond.

This win going into future rounds with Life Cocoon and Paralysis completely fresh is the compounding advantage of a fast clean kill. In longer rounds or against opponents who kite more effectively, the next layer to add is Paralysis on the enemy healer the moment Avatar goes active — locking the Holy Paladin for 4 seconds during the burst window would make this already efficient kill sequence nearly unreactable. In this round it was unnecessary, but the habit of healer-CC timing during your own burst windows will pay dividends in rounds that don't resolve this cleanly.
