The Mistweaver playing heavy_burst_swap rounds tends to absorb initial burst before committing cooldowns — this match demonstrates that pattern clearly, and also shows where the latency catches up. The round lasted 2:44, ran to 52% dampening, and ended with the Fire Mage dying at 2:43 to Wailing Arrow (119k) plus pet damage with the Monk trinket on a 89-second cooldown.

Four burst windows defined the round. The first opened at 0:52 when Trueshot and Bestial Wrath landed simultaneously on the Mage, dropping her from 100% to 65% in three seconds. The Monk trinket-escaped a Freezing Trap at 0:55 and successfully healed through it — the Mage was back at 100% by 0:58. The second Combustion window at 1:21 generated the most dangerous 90-second stretch of the match; the Monk held Leg Sweep until 1:23 when the Mage was at 39% and Combustion was running, landing on both hunters and creating genuine offensive pressure that brought the BM Hunter to 14% and the MM Hunter to 58%. This was the match's best Leg Sweep usage.

Life Cocoon was used at 0:23 (first charge) when the Warrior was at 90% and under mild opening pressure — a protective investment that cost the charge 120 seconds before it was critically needed. The second charge fired correctly at 2:05 when the Warrior hit 32% with Bestial Wrath and Explosive Shot overlapping, but 52% dampening cut the shield's value roughly in half. From 2:33 onward the third Bestial Wrath + Trueshot opened: the Monk had Paralysis, Leg Sweep, and Life Cocoon all ready at 2:26, used Leg Sweep on only the MM Hunter at 2:26, then entered the 2:33 window without a stun. The Mage sat at 15–22% for seven seconds from 2:36 while Blinding Light and Scorpid Venom and Spider Venom chained on the Monk, trinket unavailable, until Trueshot fired at 2:41 and a Wailing Arrow killed the Mage.

The context of heavy_burst_swap is important here. These rounds by nature produce the highest CD response latency — absorbing initial burst before committing cooldowns is structurally correct. What this round illustrates is the ceiling of that approach at 52% dampening: each delayed defensive use is more costly because the healer cannot recover the HP afterward. The Monk's 0:42 Paralysis was used freecast during a recovery phase rather than saved for an enemy cooldown window; in a simpler burst comp this is acceptable, but against synchronized Trueshot + Bestial Wrath, every CC charge needs to be mentally pre-assigned to disrupting that specific combo. The Leg Sweep at 2:26 on a single hunter seven seconds before the synchronized window opened was the concrete mistake — one more second of patience and that Leg Sweep lands on both hunters as Bestial Wrath fires, disrupting the kill window directly rather than before it.

The cleanse at 1:03 removing the debuff off the Mage at 53% during the first Trueshot window demonstrated the Monk's proactive utility instincts. The offense at 0:25 with Intimidating Shout on the Paladin at 100% HP was lower priority given both hunters had just used major cooldowns — targeting an enemy who is already on cooldown and at full health uses CC resources without creating a kill threat. Against this composition the Paladin should only be CCed when your own team is creating a kill window on a hunter, not proactively to interrupt healing when the hunters are already doing the most damage. Overall this was a close match that illustrates how 52% dampening converts what would be recoverable mistakes into fatal ones.
