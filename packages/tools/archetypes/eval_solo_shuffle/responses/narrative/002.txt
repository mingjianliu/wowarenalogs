The fast_passive_tunnel archetype ends rounds before conventional setup can develop, and this 35-second loss is the clearest possible example. The enemies locked onto the Arms Warrior at the opening bell and killed them with Avatar and Die by the Sword both still active — the Warrior never had a chance to convert those defensive CDs into survival because the incoming damage rate outpaced the Paladin's response window.

The enemy sequenced perfectly: Dragon's Breath on the Beast Mastery Hunter at 0:17 removed the primary peel threat, then Fire Mage Combustion at 0:29 layered with Marksmanship Aimed Shot (181k) and Black Arrow (74k) in the final 10 seconds drove the Warrior from 97% to dead in under 10 seconds. The 0.61M spike tagged in the 10 seconds ending at 0:25 shows the damage was already accumulating before the Paladin's responses landed.

Aura Mastery at 0:20 into Combustion shows the Paladin was tracking the enemy CD, but Aura Mastery reduces magic damage received by nearby allies — it doesn't absorb physical Aimed Shot hits. At 0:29, with the Warrior at 73% and the Paladin at 100% with no incoming pressure, Blessing of Sacrifice was the correct call: routing damage to the Paladin would have extended the Warrior's effective HP by several seconds and closed the gap between incoming burst and outgoing healing. The Paladin did not use it.

The round ended when the Paladin burned their trinket at 0:33 to break a 1-second Freezing Trap while the Warrior was at 36% and actively dying. Lay on Hands or Blessing of Protection in that same GCD would have saved the Warrior's life — both were on zero cooldown. The trinket decision at 0:33 is the single correctable choice in this round: save the trinket, spend an immunity or full-heal instead. In fast_passive_tunnel rounds the healer's instinct must be to spend the immunity toolkit early on the first lethal HP bracket rather than waiting for the damage to feel "certain."
