This is a Mistweaver Monk heavy_burst_tunnel archetype — the defining characteristic is that your comp generates the highest peak burst in the game but demands strong CD discipline to survive enemy windows until you can land your kills. Your team demonstrated exactly that: surviving the opening triple-CD threat and winning through repeated Mage burst sequences. But two execution habits need attention.

The opening threat window (0:09–0:33: Bloodlust + Ascendance + Avatar, 0.55M on the Mage) was the defining defensive challenge. Your Shadowfury into the Shaman at 0:15 was right — slowing the Shaman's damage flow and disrupting their position during Ascendance is the correct CC target. But Mortal Coil into the Arms Warrior at 0:21 (58% HP, still in Avatar) was a CC thrown at a fading threat. Avatar ends at roughly 0:22–0:23, and the Warrior's damage falls off naturally. In that moment, your Coil was worth far more into the Shaman, who was still freely pumping damage at 78% HP with Ascendance active. A Coil on the Shaman at 0:21 interrupts their healing amplification and gives your Mage two more seconds to survive at 62% HP.

At 0:49, Dark Pact fired while your Mage was falling from 54% HP downward. Dark Pact only heals you. Your Mage was the one being killed. This is a tension that Affliction Warlocks frequently hit: Dark Pact feels like a lifeline, but in a burst window when your squishier teammate is dying, burning a global on self-healing is effectively a wasted defensive action. The Monk identified the priority correctly and used Life Cocoon at 0:53. Your job in that same 3-second window was to CC the Arms Warrior — the player actively landing the critical damage. A Shadowfury into the Warrior at 0:48–0:50 would have bought 3 seconds of reduced damage on your Mage and potentially negated the need for the Mage's trinket at 0:52.

The CC chain at 1:22–1:24 — Shadowfury catching the Warrior and Shaman simultaneously, followed immediately by Leg Sweep — was the best coordination sequence in the match. The Mage's 0.74M burst window from 1:28 directly resulted from that setup, pushing the enemy Warrior to 30% HP. The Evoker survived because their healing was enough to maintain the Warrior and Shaman — a third CC layer in that window would have been lethal, but that's a coordination ceiling, not an individual error.

Mortal Coil at 2:12 into the Evoker at 14% was perfectly placed — delaying healing by 2 seconds while the Mage finished the kill is exactly what that ability is for. The Monk's Leg Sweep at 2:20 to lock down the surviving enemies and begin the mop-up demonstrates the kind of decisive CD usage this archetype is built around: reactive burst execution rather than scripted setup.

The core habit to reinforce: when an enemy burst window is active, CC the player who is actively dealing damage to your falling teammate, not the one whose damage is winding down. And treat Dark Pact as a last resort — use it after your CC tools are spent, not instead of them.
