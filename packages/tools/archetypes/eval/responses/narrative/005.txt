Dampening grind matches against Enhancement Shaman comps are decided by two things: whether you can survive their CD rotations without burning too deep into your own resources, and whether you dispel Flame Shock before it turns into free extra damage. You did the first well. You did the second poorly, and it almost cost you several times.

The match had three distinct Shaman burst windows. The first at 0:32 was the most dangerous — Ascendance plus Bloodlust overlapping with Avatar and Avenging Crusader already active — and you handled it cleanly. Mass dispel at 0:33 cleared Capacitor Totem, Flame Shock, and Earthbind off your DH simultaneously, which was the right call. Obsidian Scales on the Rogue covered the secondary pressure as the DH was spiking through 1.30M damage. The key is that you kept your major defensive cooldowns in reserve here. Rewind, Emerald Communion, Stasis — none of them went out. That restraint is what made the later sections of the match winnable.

The second Ascendance at 2:43 found the Rogue at 29% HP after the Rogue used Cloak. You responded by pouring healing into the Rogue and keeping everyone functional through the Ascendance window, then correctly held Emerald Communion for the moment at 3:39 when you and the DH simultaneously dropped below 55-60% under a third Bloodlust. That's textbook cooldown triage in a dampening match: you assessed which fire was most dangerous and used the tool when it actually needed to be used, not as a reflex to any low HP.

Deep Breath at 4:09 was the kill enabler. Your team had been dealing with back-to-back Champion's Spear chains that kept the DH and yourself disrupted. Deep Breath created the displacement and pressure needed to finish the Warrior, who died at 4:16. The Shaman followed at 4:22.

Here's the problem: Flame Shock. Top Preservation Evokers in this archetype maintain roughly a 30% missed cleanse rate — that's already suboptimal, but they compensate with higher healing throughput and faster CD reaction. Your missed Flame Shocks in this match ran for 23–36 second durations, with five separate instances. The worst one on yourself at 3:37 allowed 618k damage to land before you cleared it. That's not a Flame Shock tick — that's the Shaman's entire burst window worth of bonus damage from a single dispellable debuff you had time to remove. In dampening grind matches the Shaman's entire offensive strategy is to stack debuffs and push through attrition. When you leave Flame Shock up you're giving their comp an extra damage source for free.

The corrective habit is simple: every time you cast a filler heal on yourself or any ally, check their debuff frames. If Flame Shock is there and less than 3 seconds old, the next cast is a dispel. If it's been up longer, the next cast is absolutely a dispel. Make it as automatic as keeping HoTs up.

Two Sleep Walks hit Grounding Totem. That's going to happen occasionally, but both at 1:00 and 1:03 suggests you weren't tracking the totem. When you're running into the Paladin to Sleep Walk, scan the floor first. If there's a totem glowing under them, the Rogue needs to destroy it or you need to bait the totem with another ability before the CC attempt.

Overall this was a disciplined performance in a match that easily could have spiraled. The second Ascendance window at 2:43 with the Rogue at 29% is the kind of moment where healers panic-spend everything and then have nothing for the next wave. You didn't. That's the right instinct. Now just clean up the cleanse discipline.
