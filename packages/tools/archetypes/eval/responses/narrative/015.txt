Short_passive_swap losses against Restoration Shaman comps have a recognizable signature: the match goes long because your cleanse work keeps everyone alive, but the Shaman's sustain is enough to undo everything your team builds toward, and eventually attrition catches up to your team. This 3:47 match followed that template exactly.

The Affliction Warlock plus Assassination Rogue is a machine for stacking passive damage. DoTs, poisons, Haunt, Unstable Affliction — all of it ticks constantly and layers on top of physical damage from the Rogue. Against Restoration Shaman, this machine runs because the Shaman can top off the Rogue and the Warlock faster than your team can kill them. Against Restoration Shaman without purge access on your team, you're in a war of attrition where your only path to victory is a kill that happens faster than the Shaman can respond.

You kept the match close through exceptional cleanse discipline. Seven effects off your Druid at 0:22. Four effects off the Druid at 0:29 after being CC'd. Six effects off the Monk at 1:15 including Soul Anathema, Haunt, Corruption, and Capacitor Totem — a full warlock debuff package removed in one cast. Four effects at 1:16 off the Druid. Three effects at 1:54 including Fear off the Monk. Five off yourself at 2:37 when you were at 40% HP. Three off the Monk at 2:48. This rate of cleansing is what held your Druid alive to 3:23 — without it the Warlock's passive DoT damage would have compounded to a kill around 2:30.

The Blessing of Sacrifice choices were correct. At 0:19, your Druid was at 88% HP under a Kidney Shot stun and the Rogue was Garrote silencing them — Sacrifice onto the Druid bought a damage transfer at the right moment. At 2:19, the Druid was at 73% HP under steady downward pressure from the Warlock — Sacrifice here was a resource lever to buy your healing time to catch up.

Avenging Crusader rotations were timely. The 0:31 activation when the Shaman was at 39% HP and your Druid and Monk were both declining gave you a healing boost during the early damage phase. The 1:33 activation when the Druid was at 46% under Garrote silencing was the right window. The 2:42 activation at 73% Druid health responded to the Mortal Coil that had just hit.

Aura Mastery at 3:17 is a good call. The Monk was at 34% HP under the Deathmark window, your own Blessing of Protection had just expired, and the Warlock's Unstable Affliction and Drain Soul were the top damage sources in the Monk's final 10 seconds. Aura Mastery's Fear immunity and magic mitigation in that environment is meaningful.

The healing gap at 3:37 is the match-ending failure. For 9.7 seconds — with 6.8 seconds of that time not attributable to CC or cast-time — you were not casting on the Monk who was at 28% HP. Looking at the timeline: at 3:32 you cast on the Monk, at 3:34 you cast on the Monk again, then at 3:37 you cast Wrath on the Warlock. Then a 9.7-second gap before any healing activity. During that gap, the Monk fell from 34% to dead as Fear hit at 3:38 and Lightning Lasso at 3:38. The Wrath cast on the Warlock at 3:37 is the wrong decision — the Monk was below 35% HP, the Warlock was irrelevant to the Monk's immediate survival, and that cast replaced what should have been a heal.

The other structural note: your entire offensive pressure in this match went toward the Rogue. Every Hammer of Justice, every CC chain, every coordinated push — Rogue target. The Rogue was at 35% HP around 2:33 and at 25% around 3:31. The Shaman healed them back both times within seconds. The Warlock, who is the damage source and has almost no defensive cooldowns in this comp, was never seriously threatened.

Against Restoration Shaman without purge, killing through the Shaman's healing on the Rogue requires a coordinated burst that exceeds their heal-per-second for 2-3 seconds simultaneously. Your team's damage never created that spike. The Warlock is softer and dies faster to burst — the Incarnation windows on your Druid (0:23, 2:21) created real burst moments, but they were directed at the Rogue rather than the Warlock.

In rematches of this comp: identify the Warlock as the primary kill target, use Leg Sweep and Incapacitating Roar to CC the Rogue away from the kill, and attempt to spike the Warlock during Incarnation windows when your Druid's output is highest. The Shaman can't be in two places — if they're healing the Warlock, the Rogue is vulnerable, and vice versa.
