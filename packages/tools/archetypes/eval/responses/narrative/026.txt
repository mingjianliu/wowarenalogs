This was a war of attrition that the enemy won by cycling burst CDs faster than you could regenerate defensive ones. The Fury Warrior ran Avatar and Recklessness three times across the match — at 0:41, 1:19, and 2:02 — and each window cost you something you couldn't get back. By the time the fourth window opened at 2:45, you had nothing left.

The match opened with immediate chaos. Within 10 seconds the Fury Warrior had both offensive CDs rolling and your Shaman was already losing health. You used Wing Buffet at 0:13 and Time Dilation at 0:14 to buy breathing room, then Emerald Communion at 0:43 when the first real burst window hit. That was the right read — your trinket was stunned and the Shaman was at 44%. You burned through it cleanly and came out the other side with everyone healthy. No issue there.

The second window at 1:19 swapped the target to you. You had already used Rewind at 1:08 when the Shaman hit 43%, and that 240-second cooldown was now gone. When the Warrior's Recklessness came up again, you had Tip the Scales and personal heals but no major panic button. You burned down to 40% and stabilized. Survived, but the resource ledger was thin.

Then came the window that broke the match: 2:02, Avatar and Recklessness together with Incarnation active on the enemy druid. You dropped to 27% in two seconds. Obsidian Scales was your only answer. You got back to 90% because the single charge of Obsidian Scales plus your active heals were enough in the moment. But the problem was what happened 43 seconds earlier.

At 1:49, when the Shaman was at 61% during a moderate pressure phase, you used Stasis. The Shaman had already recovered from the second major window and the Warrior's next Avatar was 27 seconds away. That Stasis use was defensive by instinct, not by necessity. Those stored healing charges would have been the difference-maker in the 2:02 window when you were at 27% with the Druid's Tree of Life amplifying everything hitting you.

The enemy druid's Cyclone on your Warrior at 2:36 was the strategic killing blow. Five seconds with your damage dealer completely removed allowed their team to reset, and the Shaman's Ascendance popping simultaneously meant any counterplay was gone. After that moment, you were simply delaying. Your Warrior couldn't generate meaningful pressure, the enemy druid never went below 85%, and the fourth burst window found you with Rewind on a 199-second cooldown, Emerald Communion on a 115-second cooldown, and Stasis already spent.

The death itself was clean and expected from that point. Rampage, Bloodbath, Starsurge — standard execute rotation. Guardian would have saved you in isolation but you don't have that CD as Preservation. The lesson is about Stasis timing: in a match where you know three or more burst windows are coming, the second Stasis charge needs to survive until the window where your other defensives are depleted, not spend early keeping a teammate comfortable at 61%.
