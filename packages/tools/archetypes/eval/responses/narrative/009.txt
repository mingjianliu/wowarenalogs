Outlaw Rogue plus Frost Mage plus Holy Priest against an Assassination Rogue with a Disc Priest backing them is a race: you CC the Disc Priest out of the picture, tunnel the Rogue until they die, kill the DH cleanup. It went correctly.

The opening seconds defined everything. The enemy Assassination Rogue set the tempo aggressively — Garrote-Silence on your Mage at 0:05, then on you at 0:06. You were locked out for 4 seconds at the start of the match while they Cheap Shotted the Mage. This is the standard assassination opener and you can't prevent it, but what matters is your response: you cleansed Crippling Poison off yourself at 0:10, got Renew up on the Mage, and started building the board.

The critical sequence starts at 0:17. You Psychic Screamed the enemy Disc Priest while your Rogue had Kidney Shot queued. Psychic Scream hit the Priest and grazed a nearby target — the Priest was locked out for the duration. Meanwhile your Rogue had burned their trinket from Imprison at 0:19 and the enemy Rogue Kidney Shotted your Outlaw at 0:20 with 5 seconds of stun. You responded with Word of Glory on the Outlaw immediately, then pulled Guardian Spirit at 0:21 when the Outlaw was at 78% HP under the stun. That's the right timing for Guardian Spirit — not at full HP preemptively, but the moment your Outlaw is stunned and taking 1.14M incoming damage. The Spirit fired at 0:33 (the Buff faded marker shows Guardian Spirit expired before death), which means the Outlaw survived through the window.

At 0:22 the Disc Priest used Power Infusion and buffed the DH with it. Both Power Infusion buffs appeared as purgeable. You did not purge them. Your Mage could have purged the DH's buff in the next several seconds. Instead you correctly weighed your options and spent the global on SMite (cc target) instead — actually looking at the log, you purged Shadow Word: Pain off your Rogue at 0:37 rather than the Power Infusion off the DH. The Power Infusion expired naturally at 0:37. This is acceptable but note that Power Infusion on their DH is what allows their damage spikes. In future Disc Priest matchups where the DH is your target, purging the Power Infusion buys your DH several seconds of reduced pressure during the biggest burst window.

Divine Hymn at 0:27 was well-timed. Both the Outlaw and Mage had taken significant damage by this point, and Divine Hymn started ticking as the worst of the first burst faded. It expired at 0:29. Two casts of Divine Hymn ticked through the critical window and brought both back toward safe HP.

The second sequence — when your Mage dropped to 19% at 0:43 and the enemy Rogue popped a second Cheap Shot — was resolved by your Outlaw using Adrenaline Rush and Kidney Shot to apply pressure while you used Apotheosis at 0:47. The Mage hit Ice Block at 0:44 to buy time. Apotheosis let you pump Power Word: Shield on the Mage as they came out of Ice Block, which is exactly the right sequence: Apotheosis into Ice Block means when the Mage exits, they have a shield and you have empowered spells ready.

Power Infusion at 0:49 into both the Mage (at 69% HP) and yourself (at 79%) triggered the kill phase. The Rogue died at 0:56, the DH died at 1:05. Psychic Scream at 0:59 on the Priest kept them displaced for the cleanup on the DH.

What to note for improvement: The Blind on your Rogue at 0:09 from the enemy was notable — your Rogue used Blind at 0:09 themselves, then got Blind countered, which forced their trinket early. Track when your Rogue spends their control before committing to the kill window. The other note is the Mortal Coil / Cheap Shot sequencing in mid-match — the enemy Rogue continued to cycle control on your team while you were down Guardian Spirit. Apotheosis helped compensate, but the window between Guardian Spirit expiry (0:33) and Apotheosis (0:47) is where the Mage nearly died. That's a gap to be aware of in future cc_heavy_swap matches: when your primary defensive CD expires and the enemy CC is still cycling, that's the danger zone.

Overall this was clean execution of what your comp does. The response times were fast, the major cooldown sequencing was correct, and you didn't waste resources.
