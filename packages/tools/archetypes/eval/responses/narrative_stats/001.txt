The archetype data says this cluster has a 9.9-second median CD response latency and a 57% CD-never-used rate. Both numbers reflect rational hoarding behavior — in a game decided by CC chains, firing a CD into a stun is worse than not firing it at all. That is exactly what happened here.

At 0:58, Emerald Communion activated during a Kidney Shot. The channel was interrupted. The CD was wasted. With a 3-minute cooldown and a match that ended at 1:14, there was no second opportunity. When the mage died at 1:04, the enemy had just run their second Power Infusion window plus Deathmark, and the healer had no answer.

The 45% missed cleanse rate for this archetype is already high, and this match contributed to that number: the healer did cleanse multiple times effectively throughout (Crippling Poison, Garrote, and the 6-stack Rupture/Mutilated Flesh/Garrote/poison clear at 0:48 were all good). The one cleanse that mattered most would have been acting on the purgeable Power Infusion at 0:17 — but that is purge responsibility, not dispel, and it falls to the Frost Mage. At a benchmark purge rate of 3.48 per minute for this archetype and 13.82 CC events per minute, the game is a constant CC exchange. A free purge on PI during the first 15 seconds is one of the highest-value free actions available.

The CD timing profile (37% optimal / 8% late / 4% reactive in the cluster) suggests most top players in this archetype get their CDs out in the right window but sometimes late. Firing into a stun is worse than late — it is lost entirely. The concrete adjustment: if you activate Emerald Communion and you are stunned, the animation may not fire visually, but more importantly, train yourself to check whether you are CC'd before pressing it. In a Kidney Shot, wait 2 seconds. The DPS landing on your teammate in those 2 seconds is recoverable. The 3-minute CD is not.

The 3% healing gap rate for this archetype is excellent — healers in this cluster almost never go idle during burst. The problem in this match was not inactivity; it was a CD expended with zero output. That is a distinct failure mode worth isolating.
